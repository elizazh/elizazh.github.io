About me

Designing from lived experience

I'm a neurodivergent UX designer — which means I don't just design for neurodivergent users, I am one. That lived experience shapes every design decision I make. It's why NeuroLearn exists, and why accessibility isn't a checkbox for me, it's the foundation.

My background spans computer science, healthcare, retail management, and e-commerce entrepreneurship. I've dispensed prescriptions, managed weekend stores, and built my own business from scratch. That breadth gives me an unusual perspective: I understand users in context, not just in testing sessions.

I believe the best design happens at the intersection of empathy and rigour — where genuine understanding of human needs meets systematic, evidence-based process. That's the designer I'm becoming.

Final Year Project · Dissertation · 2025–2026

NeuroLearn

A privacy-first study tool for neurodivergent students

10–15% of UK students are neurodivergent, yet study tools like Otter, Notion, and Glean fragment their workflow across multiple platforms. NeuroLearn combines structured note summarisation, text-to-speech with live highlighting, and a Pomodoro focus timer into one calm, accessible, privacy-first web app.

Module Project · Designing Accessible Interfaces · 2025–2026

UniAccess

Accessible student support app for autistic university students

University digital systems assume students can manage fragmented platforms, tolerate cluttered dashboards, and recover easily from errors. For autistic students, these assumptions create real barriers. UniAccess combines assignments, timetable, messages, and accessibility settings into one calm, predictable mobile interface.
